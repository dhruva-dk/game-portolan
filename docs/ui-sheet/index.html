Portolan UI sheet · the old battle royale's screens (0117, ae981b6) on a 956×440 phone

Screens

    Every screen of the old game, on the phone we ship to

    Each screen of the old battle royale (worktree game-portolan-0117, commit ae981b6, 11-11 01:17) is redrawn here from its source at the iPhone 16 Pro Max's landscape page size, 956×440 CSS px. The old code was laid out for an iPad first and carried a phone layout of its own (src/chart/layout.ts, deviceClass under 500 pt tall); the mocks use that phone layout's numbers exactly. In-match mocks sit on real captures of the current game; the 0117 capture button swaps in a real headless capture of the old game at the same size, so you can check the port against what existed.

    Click any phone to see it at 1:1. Use Thumb zones to see the current game's floating stick (left 45%) and right-thumb look drag, and Touch targets to see every pressable at its drawn size (red under 44 pt; the dashed ring is the old code's enlarged hit, 56 pt). Under each screen, leave a verdict and a note; Copy all feedback puts everything on the clipboard as plain text.

    stick zone (left 45%) look drag (right 55%) safe-area insets: 62 pt at each end, 21 pt at the bottom Words in the old UI: 0 on every screen (numerals only)
    01

    Title

    src/screens/title.ts (382 lines), chart/rose.ts, chart/galleon.tsKeep

    Backdrop: current game, the ship at anchor (fly&ships=1&alt=6), under the title's thinning sheet.

    What it is. The first thing on launch. A blank sheet; the rhumb web grows from the centre, a 32-point rose draws itself, the player's galleon mark is inked beneath in gilt. The rose's needle swings while the world loads and settles north when it is ready; the sheet thins to show the world. One press anywhere and the rose flies to the lobby's career plate.

    Works

    • The needle is the loading bar. No spinner, no percentage, no words, and it means something.
    • The whole screen is one target, so it is reachable from either thumb.
    • The rose and web are the most "portolan" thing in the game: verdigris half winds, vermilion quarter winds, gilt fleur-de-lis.

    On a phone

    • The rose is 256 px across in a 440 px-tall screen. The galleon mark sits at y≈350, just inside the bottom inset. Nothing collides with the island or home indicator.
    • Nothing tells you when to press. That is fine while the needle swings; once it settles, a still screen reads as frozen. The old code had no "ready" cue beyond the needle stopping.
    • The web's hairlines (0.5 px) vanish on a 3× screen over bright sky, and the sheet's opacity has to fight the current game's bright noon.

    Rules

    Words: 0. No people. No music: the old title had wind and paper sounds only.

    Aesthetic

    Exactly on brief. It is the reference every other screen should be held to.

    Keep. Port as is. When the world is ready, add one cue: the needle gives a single small overshoot and the gilt glints. Hold the sheet a little more opaque (about 85%) over the bright noon sea.
    02

    Lobby

    src/screens/lobby.ts (642), chart/layout.ts lobbyLayout()Rework

    Backdrop: current game, a galleon at anchor (fly&ships=1&alt=6&look=-3). One finger anywhere else orbits the camera round her; two pinch.

    What it is. Home, between matches. Her ship is live at anchor, with six instruments on the view. Top left is the career plate: the level numeral in a ring that fills with gold leaf, and the rank as a small wax seal (press it for the rank folio). Top right is a ship's wheel for settings. Bottom left are four tabs: abilities, locker, rank, rivals. Bottom right, under the thumb, is the PLAY banner: a ribbon with her galleon on it.

    Works

    • PLAY is the largest thing on screen and sits right under the right thumb (centre 777, 318). It is the best-placed control in the old UI.
    • No word. By 0117 the banner carries a drawn galleon instead of the word PLAY, which the 11-10 build still wrote.
    • The ship itself is the backdrop and is touchable: orbit and pinch. It is the one 3D toy in the menus.

    On a phone

    • The four tabs are 46 pt discs 58 pt apart, bottom left, comfortable for the left thumb. But they are four ways into meta-game that a new player cannot read without opening each one.
    • The settings wheel (48 pt) is in the top-right corner, a full stretch for the right thumb. That is acceptable for settings.
    • The career plate's seal badge is 26 pt. It shares the plate's single 110×68 hit, so the press is fine, but the seal itself is too small to read its colour.
    • The backdrop drag (orbit) takes every touch outside the instruments, so a missed tab press swings the camera. That is harmless.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Levels and ranks are numerals and seals. The "rivals" tab shows two ships bow to bow, which implies other captains, but no people are drawn.

    Aesthetic

    Strong: paper laid on the world, iron ink, a wax seal, a ribbon banner. The tabs' rounded-crown slips read more like a mobile tab bar than a chart. Fine, but the weakest drawing here.

    Rework. Keep the plate, the wheel and the banner. Cut the tab row to what survives the meta decisions (Q2 below); if only abilities survives, it becomes a single seal beside the banner showing the ability worn. The lobby then has four things on it.

    Folio · Abilities

    src/screens/folio.ts #abilities(), screens/cards.tsRework

    The folio sheet docks right under the wheel: 535×336, window inset 40, so 455×256 of content shows. 8 actives over 8 passives; a card pressed is worn at once.

    What it is. The sheet the burst tab unrolls. It holds two rows of woodcut cards: actives (repair, smoke, ram, reveal, hard turn, decoy, wave, drift mine) under a burst head, and passives under a lantern head. The worn card has a gilt seal; a locked card is faint, with the level that unlocks it.

    Works

    • Wordless cards: a woodcut and, if locked, a numeral. That is the right grammar.
    • One press wears a card. No confirm step.

    On a phone

    • The phone layout gives each card 212 pt, so only 1⅓ cards show at a time. Choosing among 16 means scrolling about eight rows on a 256 pt window.
    • Run at phone size, the old flow test could not scroll the long folio lists ("a drag on the sheet scrolls the long list" failed). The window is too short for momentum to help.
    • No hint of what an ability does: the woodcut alone must carry it. Ram (hull + arrow) and hard turn (curved arrow) are guessable; deep hold, salvage and quick hands are not.

    Rules

    Words: 0. "Quick hands" is drawn as a hand icon, a human sign (see Q1).

    Aesthetic

    Faithful to the chart look: card stock, hatching, fold lines and a coffee ring on the sheet.

    Rework. If abilities stay, show both rows at once as small 56 pt seals, 8 across (fits 455 pt), with the chosen one large on the left of the sheet. Each card's effect is taught by a short looping woodcut animation when pressed, not by text.

    Folio · Locker

    src/screens/folio.ts #locker(), cards.ts; six kinds: sail colour, sail pattern, hull paint, figurehead, emblem, wakeCut

    Sail colours shown. Unlock levels 2, 4, 6 are inked under the locked cards; the worn one has the gilt seal.

    What it is. Cosmetics, opened from the chest tab. Six groups of cards, each under a small head drawing; the worn card in each group is sealed. Unlocks come by level (a numeral) or by rank tier (a wax seal).

    Works

    • Swatches are drawn as little sails, hulls and wakes: the clearest cards in the folios.
    • Lock state is legible without words: faint ink plus a level numeral.

    On a phone

    • Four 100 pt cards a row, so 1¾ rows visible of a list six groups long. Scrolling is the whole interaction.
    • A "worn" change has no preview: the ship behind the sheet changes, but the sheet covers the ship's right half.
    • In the old flow test at phone size, "a press on an unlocked card changes her look" failed.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Figureheads (heron, whale, ammonite, narwhal tusk, coral antler, sunburst, plain stem) are carved work, a mild sign of a carver.

    Aesthetic

    On style. The card stock and rarity rules (silver, verdigris, azure, vermilion, gold) carry over from the loot.

    Cut for the first build. Cosmetics are a retention system, not part of the core loop, and this screen is mostly scrolling. If they return, put them on the ship: tap her sail in the lobby to cycle sail colours in place, with no sheet.

    Folio · Rank

    src/screens/folio.ts #rank(), #staffOfTiers()Rework

    Opened from the pennants tab or the career plate. Eight tiers (Iron to Legend) as pennants on a staff; tiers not reached are faint. Matches sailed (ship) and wins (gold star, red figure) on the right.

    What it is. Where you stand. A staff flies eight pennants, one per tier, with your seal at your tier's hoist and a gold star at your best. On the right are two big numerals, matches and wins. Below the staff, the cosmetics each tier brings.

    Works

    • The staff of pennants is a beautiful, wordless ladder, and the most chart-like folio.
    • Two numerals carry the career; that is the right amount.

    On a phone

    • The staff body is 340 pt tall in a 256 pt window, so the bottom tiers and your own seal are below the fold (a new player is Iron, tier 0). The capture shows the staff without the one thing it is for.
    • The tier colours (pale wash on paper) are hard to tell apart when faint: violet, silver and diamond-blue run together.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Ranks imply a league of people; drawn as pennants, they read as a ladder. Acceptable.

    Aesthetic

    On style. Pennants and a staff with a gilt finial are period-correct chart ornaments.

    Rework. Fold rank into the career plate and the result sheet; do not give it its own folio. If kept, lay the staff horizontally across the sheet (pennants hanging down, 8 across 455 pt) so the whole ladder and your seal fit without scrolling.

    Folio · Rivals

    src/screens/folio.ts #rivals(), #rivalCard()Cut

    Each league captain (a bot) as a card: her galleon in her colours, her emblem on the sail, her rank's seal, her two abilities, and your record against her (ships of hers you sank / ships of yours she sank).

    What it is. A roster of the 19 bot captains you meet, with a head-to-head record per captain. Opened from the two-ships tab.

    Works

    • The coloured galleons with emblems are charming and make each bot recognisable on the full map later.

    On a phone

    • Two cards a row at 214×133 pt: about 3 cards visible out of 19.
    • Each card packs 6 marks (ship, seal, two abilities, two tallies) at 20–30 pt. The tallies' icons differ only by ink colour, which is illegible at this size.

    Rules

    Words: 0. But this screen is about captains: persistent persons with records. That is the closest the old UI comes to depicting people.

    Aesthetic

    On style, though six marks to a card is busy for a chart.

    Cut. It is information without a decision attached. Keep the emblems: they belong on the full map and in the hit feedback, where they help you tell ships apart.
    07

    Settings

    src/screens/folio.ts #settings(), app/settings.tsKeep

    Ten controls in two columns of five rows (92 pt each); three rows show. Hidden below: ambience, invert look, interface volume, auto-fire.

    What it is. The wheel's sheet. "The settings, in words nowhere": each control is a woodcut plus a bar or a pip. Stick side (a frame with the stick and a hand), haptics, master volume, shake, effects volume, look sensitivity, ambience, invert look, interface volume, auto-fire.

    Works

    • Fully wordless settings that still read: speaker plus cannon is effects, speaker plus gust is ambience. Clever and in style.
    • The stick-side control is a little picture of the screen with the stick on the side it will be.
    • No music setting, because there is no music.

    On a phone

    • Only 6 of 10 controls are visible. In the old flow test at phone size, invert look, auto-fire, ambience and interface volume could not be reached ("no such press"): the bottom two rows were unreachable.
    • Bars are 111 pt long and 26 pt tall; the hit is the whole 215×92 row, which is good. Pips (on/off) are 20 pt drawn, inside the same row hit.
    • The haptics and shake icons are hands. See Q1.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Hands drawn ×3 (stick side, haptics, shake).

    Aesthetic

    On style. The azure, sienna and ochre washes in the bars echo the chart's colourist.

    Keep the wordless approach; fix the fit. Cut it to the 6 controls a phone needs (stick side, master, effects, ambience, sensitivity, haptics) in three columns of two rows so all of them show. Fold invert look into sensitivity (drag past zero) and drop auto-fire.

    The Gale · the drop

    src/screens/gale.ts (287), screens/minimap.ts, screens/scraps.tsKeep

    Backdrop: current game from 160 m (fly&alt=160&look=-50&ships=8). The old Gale carried the fleet high over the planet on a wind; DIVE is faint for the first 2.8 s.

    What it is. The battle royale's drop. The fleet is carried on a gale over the planet. At the top centre, the seconds the Gale has left; the disc shows the Gale's dotted course, the fleet and the Storm's first circle. DIVE, under the right thumb, is a slip with her ship stooping down a gust. After the dive, the stick comes up for the glide and her marker (a vermilion pennant on a staff) stands on the water.

    Works

    • DIVE sits exactly where Fire will be. The thumb learns the spot before the match starts.
    • Wordless: the 11-10 build still said "DIVE"; 0117 draws it.
    • The countdown slip is one numeral, legible at 32 pt.

    On a phone

    • The first-drop teaching slip (118×128, a hand tapping down) is pinned over the minimap disc: it spans y 127–255 and the disc reaches y 160 (see the "First drop" state and its capture).
    • The DIVE drawing is a 128×102 slip. The gust curls and the ship are each about 40 pt, and the ship stooping is hard to read at a glance.
    • The disc press opens the full map, but the disc sits inside the look-drag zone. A look drag that starts on the disc opens the map on release.

    Rules

    Words: 0. The teaching hand is a human sign (Q1). The Gale is a fixed per-match carrier, not a scripted event; it is the genre's drop.

    Aesthetic

    On style; the pennant marker on the water is a lovely chart gesture.

    Keep. Move the first-drop hint off the disc: pulse DIVE's own slip, three ink rings radiating from it, with no hand. Make the DIVE drawing simpler and bigger: the ship alone, bow down, filling the slip.

    Full map

    src/screens/fullmap.ts (420), screens/planet.ts (336)Keep

    Backdrop: current game from orbit (fly&alt=1100&look=-89), slid left under a square window cut in the sheet, as the old code slid the world.

    What it is. Opened by pressing the minimap disc, over the drop or the match, which go on underneath. The sheet covers the whole screen with a square window on the live planet, as tall as the screen, north up. Coasts are inked over it, with the Storm's edge and next circle, every ship in range by her emblem (yours gilt) and your marker pennant. Down the right are ships afloat, your kills, your damage and the Storm's clock. One finger spins the planet, two pinch from the whole planet down to a bay, and a tap sets the marker. Tap outside or on the disc to close.

    Works

    • The live planet seen through a cut window is the old game's best idea. It is the globe as a chart.
    • Four readings with woodcut icons and numerals: the standings without a word.
    • Spin, pinch and tap are honest phone gestures.

    On a phone

    • The window is ~430 pt square, 45% of the screen width. On a 6.9" phone the whole planet is about 7 cm across. Enough.
    • The match goes on under it, but the stick and fire are gone while it is open, so opening it in a fight is a trap.
    • Closing is "tap outside the window or on the disc". The disc is a 150 pt target top right, but nothing marks it as a close button.
    • The readings' icons (kills vs damage) are hard to tell apart at 22 pt.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Sheet, web, square frame with a graduated band: on brief.

    Aesthetic

    The most convincing "16th-century map" moment in the match.

    Keep. Keep the stick live while the map is open (left thumb still steers), and close it on a second tap of the disc, drawn as the disc rolled up into the corner. Cut the damage row; three readings are enough.

    Match HUD · at sea

    src/screens/hud.ts (872), screens/instruments.ts, screens/minimap.ts, chart/layout.tsRework

    Backdrop: current game sailing (drive=0,1,12). Positions are chartLayout(956, 440, safe 62/62/20). Left-handed mirrors the six controls; the readings stay put.

    What it is. The match. Top left: the vitals slip, shield bar (azure) over hull bar (her colour), each with its numeral. Top centre: the Storm's clock (spiral + m:ss) and ships afloat (ship + count). Top right: the minimap disc, with the region's woodcut inking in on entering a region. Under the left thumb: the floating stick (resting ring) and the sails lever above it (furled, half, full). Under the right thumb: Fire (cannon), with Gust (pips for charges), Ability and Reload round it. Bottom centre: the hotbar's five slots. Centre: the reticle. Below the disc: the gull's hail seal.

    Works

    • Genre-standard placement (the code cites issue #54): readings along the top, controls under the thumbs, hotbar between them. A battle-royale player is at home.
    • One visual family: every instrument is paper laid on the world, ruled in iron, with a woodcut.
    • The right cluster is laid on an arc the thumb sweeps without moving the hand (ability 106 pt and reload 113 pt from Fire).
    • Everything pressable has at least a 56 pt hit (TARGET), though some are drawn smaller.

    On a phone

    • 16 things on screen before a fight starts: vitals, clock, afloat, disc, seal, sails, stick, 5 slots, fire, gust, ability, reload, reticle. It is an iPad HUD shrunk, not a phone HUD.
    • Stick-zone collisions: slots 1–2 (x 299–405) and the sails lever (x 132–188) lie inside the current game's stick zone (left 45%, x < 430). The chart takes touches first, so a thumb landing there for the stick hits a slot or the sails instead.
    • Look-zone collisions: every right-thumb button sits in the look zone, as does the disc (150 pt). A look drag that starts on Fire fires.
    • The hail seal (704, 131) is high in the right thumb's stretch zone: unreachable mid-fight without regripping.
    • Lead-white marks (stick ring, empty slots, reticle) were drawn for the old night sea. Over the current game's bright noon water they disappear (toggle the 0117 capture: it is at night).
    • The sails lever duplicates the current stick, which already sets speed by how far it is pushed.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Numerals: 0, 100, 1:21, 20, 8. No people drawn on the calm HUD.

    Aesthetic

    Consistent and handsome as a family. The paper slips' drop shadows make it read as cards floating over water more than ink on a chart, which is the one stylistic tension.

    Rework. Phone HUD at 9 things: vitals, clock and afloat merged into one top slip; disc; stick (no sails lever); Fire; Ability; Gust; a hotbar of 3 slots placed right of centre, out of the stick zone (x 470–640). Reload becomes automatic (a drain ring on Fire). The gull is cut (see 14). Every lead-white mark gets an iron halo so it holds on bright water.

    HUD · in a fight

    src/screens/hud.ts #combat(), #incoming(), #vitalsAndHurts(), #hitFigures()Keep

    Backdrop: current game with a fleet ahead (ships=6). The figures and arcs are drawn at the moment they are brightest; all fade within 0.9–1.1 s.

    What it is. What a fight adds. When your shot lands, a white cross flashes at the reticle and a red damage figure rises from the ship struck (larger for larger hits, gold when it sinks her). Red arcs at the screen's edge point toward each ship that hit you, and a pulsing arc marks a heavy gun winding up. A row of hurts (sail, mast, rudder, leak, fire, ice) sits under the vitals while each lasts. Reload shows a draining red ring, ability cooldown a ring and seconds, and Fire a ring that fills as the deck steadies.

    Works

    • The arcs: direction of threat without a word, at the edge, out of the play area.
    • Damage figures in vermilion with a white halo read over sea and sky, and gold for the sink is a reward.
    • Hurts are woodcuts that appear only while true. That is good economy.
    • The steadiness ring on Fire teaches the swell mechanic in place.

    On a phone

    • Four ring indicators (steady, reload, ability, gust pips) cluster under one thumb. The thumb covers the ring it is pressing, so its own feedback is hidden.
    • Hurt discs are 28 pt with 20 pt woodcuts: legible as shapes, not as which part is hurt.
    • The arc ellipse sits 4% inside the edges, so on the left the arc runs through the safe inset and under the stick.

    Rules

    Words: 0. Numerals only.

    Aesthetic

    Vermilion rubric for harm and gold for triumph: exactly the chartmaker's two accent inks.

    Keep the marks. Move the steadiness ring from Fire to the reticle (it is where the eye is). Keep the incoming arcs inside the safe area. Show hurts as at most 3 at once, at 36 pt.

    HUD · hotbar and loot

    src/screens/hud.ts #hotbar(), #offer(), #slotDown/#slotUp/#dropHeld; instruments.ts slot()Rework

    Slots are 50 pt squares 6 pt apart (hit 56). A tap chooses; tap the chosen one to use a consumable; hold 0.6 s to throw it over the side. With the hold full and loot in reach, a hand points at the chosen slot: tap it to swap.

    What it is. Loot. Eight weapons (cannon, scatter, harpoon, mortar, repeater, broadside, chain, heated) by rarity ink, and four consumables (small and big shield, repair kit, full repair) with counts. The chosen slot is gilt. A consumable in use runs a gold line along the slot's top. A held slot draws a vermilion line round its square until it drops.

    Works

    • Wordless swapping: the hand over the chosen slot when the hold is full says "this one goes".
    • Hold-to-drop draws its own progress round the square. Discoverable once seen.
    • Counts are numerals in the slot corner.

    On a phone

    • 5 slots × 50 pt = 274 pt of bottom edge, and 2 of them sit in the stick zone (see 10). A thumb reaching for the stick near the bottom chooses slot 1 instead.
    • The offer hand (40 pt) hovers above slot 1, right beside the resting stick, in the busiest part of the left thumb's area.
    • Swapping needs the right thumb to leave the fire cluster and travel 250–480 pt left, mid-fight.
    • Weapons are told apart only by woodcut at 31 pt. Scatter, chain and broadside are near-identical at that size.

    Rules

    Words: 0. The pointing hand is a human sign (Q1).

    Aesthetic

    Slots on paper chits, gilt for chosen: on style. Empty slots as lead-white outlines are the least chart-like mark in the HUD.

    Rework. Three slots, right of centre (above Reload's old spot), so the right thumb reaches them without crossing. Pick up by sailing over loot: the swap takes the chosen slot automatically, undoable by tapping the dropped item's mark within 3 s. Replace the hand with the slot's own square inking red.

    HUD · the Storm

    src/screens/hud.ts #stormClock(), #stormRim(); icons/sets/hud.ts stormSpiral()Keep

    Backdrop: current game at dusk (t=18.6&ships=4). The Storm is the shrinking circle; outside it, the hull wears down.

    What it is. The battle royale's circle. The clock's spiral winds tighter in four steps as the circle closes, and the figure turns vermilion while it shrinks. Outside the circle the screen's rim is washed red, deeper the farther out. On the disc, the Storm's edge and the next circle.

    Works

    • The spiral that winds tighter is a wordless, period-plausible clock face. Excellent.
    • The red rim is unmissable without covering the centre.

    On a phone

    • The rim is an ellipse at 62% of the half-screen, so on a 2.17:1 phone the red reaches well into the stick and fire areas at full depth. That is fine as a wash, but the controls lose contrast.
    • The clock's four spiral steps are only 1–2 pt apart at 24 pt; nobody will read them.
    • No direction to safety on the main view: you must read the disc.

    Rules

    Words: 0. The Storm circle is a rule of the genre, not a scripted event.

    Aesthetic

    The rim is a screen-space vignette, not a chart mark. Acceptable as "the sea bleeding red".

    Keep. Add a vermilion edge arc toward the circle's centre while outside (the same mark as incoming fire, in a different weight). Let the spiral's tightening go; the red figure says "closing" on its own.

    HUD · hailing the gull

    src/screens/hud.ts #hail(), gullDevice(), tallyMarks(); chart/seal.tsCut

    The seal (25 pt radius) at the disc's lower left; the coins' tally slip (104×28) beside it, one stroke per 5 coins. The seal's illumination shows the best parcel the coins buy: plain ink, rubric, red-and-blue, gold.

    What it is. A delivery. Coins picked up at sea buy a parcel (supplies, armament, treasure) that a gull flies to you. A wax seal with a gull on it is the button; its wax colour says which parcel you can afford. While the gull is away the seal is plain and faint. The first time, a hand points up at the seal.

    Works

    • Wax-seal illumination as a price tier is a clever, wordless idea.
    • Tally marks for coins are period-perfect.

    On a phone

    • The seal is at (704, 131): top of the right thumb's stretch, a regrip mid-match.
    • The seal is always drawn, black, even with no coins. It is a black disc on the HUD that does nothing for most of the match (see the calm capture).
    • A second economy (coins) on top of loot is a lot to teach on a 6-inch screen.

    Rules

    Words: 0. But coins, a tally and "treasure" are signs of people: money is made by someone. A hand points at the seal on first use (Q1).

    Aesthetic

    The seal is on style. The tally slip is one more paper chit in an already crowded top right.

    Cut. Drop coins and the gull delivery. If supply drops matter, let gulls drop crates at sea that anyone can sail to: the same idea with no button and no money.

    Teaching scraps

    src/screens/scraps.ts (106), app/tips.ts; placed by layout.ts scrapPlace()Rework

    The scrap's phone box is computed as 78×54 pt at (113, 141): what fits between the vitals' hurt row and the sails lever. The inset (drawn by the sheet, not the game) shows it 3×.

    What it is. Wordless tutorial. The first time a mechanic matters, a drawn slip pins to the left edge for 6.9 s and dries out, once per career. Wind: a ship head to wind with a dotted course tacking up through it. Fight: a hull with a shot's dotted arc coming in. Closing: the Storm spiral with a hand tracing it inward. It takes no touch.

    Works

    • Teaching by drawing, once, in place, with no pause and no button: right for a wordless game.
    • Remembered per career, so it never nags.

    On a phone

    • 78×54 pt is too small for a diagram: the ship is 19 pt, the dotted course 1 pt dots. Compare the capture: it reads as a smudge.
    • It sits in the stick zone, above the sails lever, where the left thumb's knuckle covers it.
    • 6.9 s on screen, during the moment it teaches (a fight, the circle closing), when the eye is elsewhere.

    Rules

    Words: 0. The closing scrap uses a tracing hand (Q1). The wind scrap's course is a ship sailing itself: fine.

    Aesthetic

    A pinned slip with a pin glint: on style.

    Rework. Pin scraps top-centre under the clock at 160×110 pt, out of both thumb zones, and draw them before the moment (the first time the Storm's clock appears, not when it closes). The closing scrap animates its dotted line instead of a hand.

    Spectating

    src/screens/hud.ts spectate(), #watchOnly(); app/app.ts (HOLD.spectate = 3)Keep

    Backdrop: current game, a fleet (ships=6). After she sinks, the controls and vitals go; the disc (rimmed gilt, the watched ship's arrow gilt) and the Storm's clock stay. A tap anywhere skips to the result.

    What it is. After sinking: the camera follows another ship, and the HUD is pared to the clock and the disc, gilt so you know it is not you. One tap anywhere skips.

    Works

    • The right amount of UI: two readings, and the whole screen is a 956×440 skip button.
    • Gilt on the disc as "this is someone else" is a quiet, consistent signal.

    On a phone

    • Nothing says you sank or by whom. The 0117 capture shows the moment: a planet, some debris, a clock.
    • A whole-screen skip means a nervous thumb (still on the stick zone) skips by accident the instant the ship sinks.

    Rules

    Words: 0.

    Summary

    The shared visual language, as it existed

    One kit, one hand. Everything is drawn on a single canvas chart layer by src/chart/* (~6k lines): a quill pen with three line weights, parchment with grain and burnt rims, iron-gall ink that multiplies into the paper, and bodied pigments laid over it. On the phone the weights are hair 0.5, fine 0.9, bold 1.6 pt (iPad 0.6/1.05/1.9).

    Lettering. No letters anywhere at 0117. Numerals are drawn by chart/numerals.ts in an italic hand at three body heights on the phone: 18 (hit figures), 16 (clocks), 44 (result and DIVE). The mocks use EB Garamond italic with lining figures as the stand-in.

    Instruments. screens/instruments.ts defines the HUD family. A slip is a hand-cut paper rectangle with a shadow. A disc is a paper round with a bold ring and a hairline inside. A bar is a ruled strip with a wash and a numeral. A badge is a woodcut plus a numeral on a slip. A slot is a chit with an inked square, gilt when chosen. The stick is a lead-white ring with a paper nub, and the reticle a white cross over its ink shadow. Wax seals come in five illuminations (ink, rubric, red-and-blue, silver, gold).

    Icons. 116 drawings on a 24-unit grid (src/icons/sets/*), cut as woodcuts by chart/woodcut.ts: solid shapes are a firm outline round close hatching, crossed on the shadow side, with cuts left as bare paper.     Cross-screen problems

    1. It is an iPad UI with a phone layout, not a phone UI. The README says "for iPad"; the phone layout shrinks the same 16-item HUD and 5-folio lobby into 440 pt. Every folio shows 1–2 rows of a long list; settings hide 40% of their controls.
    2. Pressables in the stick zone. The old stick zone was the left 42%; the current game's is 45%. Hotbar slots 1–2, the sails lever and the teaching scrap's area all sit inside it, and the chart takes touches first.
    3. Every right-hand control is in the look zone. There is no reserved strip for look; a drag that starts on any button presses it.
    4. Lead white was tuned for night. 0117 matches often started at dusk or night (random start hour); the current game is bright noon. White marks need an ink halo or they vanish.
    5. Paper-card density. Up to 14 paper chits float over the sea at once. The chart look is strongest where there is less paper (title, full map, result).
    6. The manicule. A drawn pointing hand appears in the Gale hint, the closing scrap, the loot offer, the hail hint and three settings icons. It is the one recurring human sign.
    7. Meta-game breadth. Levels, 8 rank tiers, 16 abilities, 6 cosmetic kinds, 19 rivals and coins: a large retention layer around a core that is not built yet.
    8. Icon pairs too alike at phone size: kills/damage, scatter/chain/broadside, rank tier washes when faint.

    Open decisions

    1. Is a drawn hand a "sign of people"? Recommend yes. Replace every manicule with ink rings, a dotted course or the target itself pulsing.
    2. How much meta-game? (levels, rank, abilities, cosmetics, rivals) Recommend: level ring and one ability choice only for now. Cut locker, rivals and the rank folio; rank lives on the plate.
    3. Keep coins and the gull delivery? Recommend cut: money is a sign of people, and it adds a button at the worst reach.
    4. How many hotbar slots on the phone? Recommend 3, right of centre, out of the stick zone.
    5. Keep the sails lever? Recommend cut: the current stick already sets speed by push.
    6. Manual reload or automatic? Recommend automatic, shown as a drain ring on Fire. That is one less button.
    7. Strictly zero words, or numerals plus nothing else? Recommend: keep 0117's rule of numerals only, no letters, including no region names.
    8. Should the look drag ever start on a button? Recommend: buttons win only on a tap. A touch that moves more than 10 pt within 120 ms becomes a look drag.
    9. Teaching scraps: keep? Recommend keep 3, larger, top-centre, shown before the moment rather than during it.
    10. Left-handed mirroring? Recommend keep: it is cheap in this layout (6 controls mirror, readings stay).
    11. Paper shadows (cards floating over the world) or flat ink on the view? Recommend: drop the shadow for HUD instruments and keep it for sheets (folio, map, result), so the HUD reads as marks and the sheets as paper.
